working papers

  1. 2026

    Testing the Number of Factors with an Edge-Regression Estimator of the Tracy–Widom Scale

    with Liangjun Su (Tsinghua University)

    A test for the number of factors that estimates the Tracy–Widom scale directly, by regressing near-edge eigenvalues on square-root-law quantiles; the studentised gap is pivotal and size-controlled, and beats the ratio test when factors are weak.

    Abstract

    We propose a test for the number of factors in large approximate factor models that exploits the edge of the noise spectrum. If there are k factors, the (k + 1)th and (k + 2)th largest eigenvalues of the sample covariance matrix fluctuate as the two largest eigenvalues of the noise, so their gap has a limit determined by the Tracy–Widom law, up to an unknown scale. Onatski (2009) removes this scale by a ratio of eigenvalue gaps. Following Dörnemann and Lopes (2025), we estimate it directly instead. Exploiting the square-root decay of the noise spectral density at its edge, we propose an edge-regression estimator of the scale. Our estimator regresses the eigenvalues near the edge on the corresponding quantiles of the square-root law, and is consistent at rate T−1/2+ε under both the null and the alternative. The studentized gap has a pivotal limit, so the test controls size asymptotically, and its power tends to one. Applied sequentially, the test yields an estimator of the number of factors that overestimates it with asymptotic probability at most the level, and is consistent when the level vanishes. Our method allows factors of any divergent strength, noise that is serially or cross-sectionally correlated, and, under additional conditions, exactly tied factors. In simulations, the test has size close to the nominal level and is at least as powerful as the ratio test of Onatski (2009), much more so when a factor is weak.

  2. 2026

    Which Units Act as Factors? Testing Pervasive Units in Large Panels

    with Liangjun Su (Tsinghua University) and Zhaocheng Zhang (Fudan University)

    Develops size-controlled inference for pervasive units — series that lie in the factor space — by partialling each unit out and testing whether exactly one factor is removed with an edge-regression test; delivers screening, a confidence set, and a point estimate for the pervasive units.

    Abstract

    A large panel can be driven by a few of its own members: a dominant firm, a key sector, or a traded factor that many other series follow. Such pervasive units act as observed common factors, but existing methods identify them by classification rules that provide no test with controlled size and no stated error rate. This paper develops inference for pervasive units. A unit is pervasive exactly when its series lies in the factor space. We test this by partialling the unit out of the panel and checking whether exactly one factor is removed, using an edge-regression test for the number of factors. The test has controlled size without estimating the factors, allows units and factors of any strength, and lets the cross-section grow at the same rate as the time dimension. Screening every unit gives a set that contains the pervasive units with high probability, so finding no pervasive unit becomes a conclusion at a stated level. Inverting joint tests gives a confidence set for the set of pervasive units and a point estimate. In US sectoral industrial production, no sector is pervasive. Among 196 corporate bond factors, three observed factors jointly represent the three common factors. Among 91 currencies against the dollar, the euro is the only major currency the data cannot exclude from the factor space.

    Working paper
  3. 2025

    Common Dynamics in Non-Negative Panels with Many Zeros

    with Ruofan Xu

    A score-driven dynamic factor model for non-negative panels with many zeros, using GB2 distributions with censoring and zero-augmentation; improves in-sample fit and forecasts on high-frequency trading-volume and global rainfall data.

    Abstract

    High-dimensional panels of non-negative time series with frequent zeros — daily rainfall across stations, intraday trading volume across assets, disease counts across regions — combine three features that standard dynamic factor models cannot accommodate together: a point mass at zero, heavy right tails, and strong cross-sectional co-movement. We propose a censored score-driven dynamic factor model that captures all three in a single, likelihood-based framework. Each series is generated by a generalised beta distribution of the second kind (GB2) that is left-shifted and censored at zero, following the univariate construction of Harvey and Ito (2020), while a small number of common latent factors drive the log-scale of every series and are updated recursively by the aggregated cross-sectional score. The construction inherits the robustness of the univariate censored score and the parsimony of a factor structure, and its one-step-ahead predictive likelihood is available in closed form, so no simulation or particle filtering is required. We then develop the asymptotic theory of the maximum likelihood estimator — left open in the univariate case by Harvey and Ito (2020) — by (i) bounding the per-series censored score derivative in closed form, (ii) deriving the filter Jacobian and two contraction conditions (a uniform sufficient condition and a feasible, empirically testable Lyapunov condition), and (iii) handling the discontinuity of the censored score at the zero boundary through a Tobit-style regime decomposition. Under explicit primitive assumptions we establish strong consistency and asymptotic normality of a penalised quasi-maximum-likelihood estimator, and we give a computationally tractable three-step estimator with one Newton refinement that attains joint-MLE efficiency. Standard errors follow from a regime-decomposed sandwich formula with a two-step variance correction.

  4. 2024

    Testing Amplifiers in Observed Factor Models

    with Yiyang Zhang

    Introduces "amplifier" effects — where some units' idiosyncratic noise carries explanatory power for other units' factor loadings — with a test and a selection procedure; detects the energy industry as an amplifier of the profit factor in the Fama–French model.

    Abstract

    We introduce amplifier factor models, in which the factor loadings of non-amplifier stocks are time-varying functions of the idiosyncratic shocks of designated portfolios called amplifiers. Under a channel-wise exclusion that permits cross-amplification across groups, the amplifier coefficient at a candidate channel (a, k) is estimated by a heteroskedasticity-based instrumental-variables GMM in the spirit of Lewbel (2012), which uses only data already in the model and is consistent under cross-sectional endogeneity between non-amplifier residuals and the amplifying portfolio's innovation. Amplifier candidates are generated either by economic intuition or by a row-masked Group LASSO with selection-consistency and post-selection inference; either route produces a candidate set that is verified by two panel tests — a pervasiveness test and a prevalence test — under a convergent-normalized-spectrum aggregation theory that accommodates the strong-factor residual structure typical of equity panels. An empirical illustration on U.S. equities is sketched.

    Working paper
  5. 2023

    Factor Dipping

    Sole-authored

    Proposes "factor dipping" to generate valid alternative PC estimates under the standard factor-model specification, with a specification test and a test for the number of transformation-stable factors; used as a measure of PC-estimation uncertainty.

    Abstract

    Standard principal-component (PC) factor estimation relies on a spectral gap that macroeconomic and financial panels routinely lack. This weak-factor problem, combined with the linear pre-processing transformations implicit in applied work, generates PC uncertainty that standard asymptotic intervals severely understate. We propose factor dipping: re-estimate the factors from bounded, invertible transformations of the data, then invert the transformation. Under the standard specification, the dipped and original estimates span the same space up to Op(n−2) deviations, where n is the diverging spectral gap. We turn this invariance into a specification test with a derived limiting distribution, and into a representation-sensitivity band that quantifies the misspecification-induced uncertainty weak factors inject. Applications to Stock–Watson factors, factor-augmented VAR impulse responses, three-pass risk premia, and quantile factor models show that standard intervals can mislead and downstream structural conclusions can be overturned. We recommend factor dipping as a routine diagnostic before any PC-based inference.
